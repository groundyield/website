## 3. The problem

Smallholders in southern Mozambique operate far below potential yields. Published maize figures are commonly **~0.4–1.3 t/ha** (often near **~1 t/ha** nationally) — see [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). The gap is not mainly “effort” — it is reliable water, improved inputs, storage, timely advice, and markets that pay.

Most interventions are **opaque** and non-replicable.  
Many **large commercial SA-style bets** in Mozambique also fail under cost, politics, logistics, and scale risk — the operator lived one of those failures ([LESSONS_MZ.md](LESSONS_MZ.md)).

**Charter rule:** impact claims use **local baselines**, not national averages alone.

## 4. The approach

### 4.1 Physical package (core)

Per modular unit (~0.25–0.5 ha) — [UNIT.md](UNIT.md):

| Component | Role |
|-----------|------|
| Solar irrigation | Water when rain fails · dry-season cash crops |
| Improved / hybrid seed + micro-dose fertilizer | Yield response without blanket over-application |
| Hermetic storage | Cut post-harvest loss |
| Optional poultry/goats | Income diversification |
| Phone agronomy | **After** human review gate only |

Irrigation design choices: [IRRIGATION.md](IRRIGATION.md)

Full stack map: **[SYSTEM.md](SYSTEM.md)**

### 4.3 Design principles

1. **Modular** — start small; scale by adding identical units  
2. **Transparent** — no private performance reports  
3. **Replicable** — MIT-open design; others can copy  
4. **Local first** — community legitimacy; not imported labor empires  
5. **Measured** — baseline → intervention → public results  
6. **Integrity over optics** — no ghost units, no silent rewrites  
7. **Stop before fake promises** — if funding/time dies, say so ([WHO.md](WHO.md))
