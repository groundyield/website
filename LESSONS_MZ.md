This page answers the fair question: *why should anyone trust a “day-one” GitHub pilot?*

## 1d. “Near impossible” vs modular aims (no contradiction if scoped)

Lived view: **large commercial SA-style farming** under the cost/gov/logistics conditions hit felt **structurally very hard / near impossible**.

That does **not** automatically mean:

- small modular packages cannot help **one household’s food**, or  
- measured experiments are worthless.

It **does** mean:

- Charter aims (e.g. 30–60 units, large % uplift) are **hard, unproven hypotheses** — not a claim that empire farming suddenly works.  
- Default if markets or capital fail: **few units + open autopsy** ([VALUE_AND_MONEY.md](VALUE_AND_MONEY.md) Plan B).  
- Full reconciliation language: [CHARTER.md](CHARTER.md) · [STRATEGIC_GAPS.md](STRATEGIC_GAPS.md) §4.

| Teaching **stop rules** and cost reality | Hero narrative that skips failure |

**Open-source food** here means: publish the BOM, the costs, the failures, the small unit design — so the next person does not need to lose ZAR 10m in silence to learn.

## 3. What this changes for GroundYield design

| Lesson from the loss | Design response |
|----------------------|-----------------|
| Large land + cattle empire is capital- and politics-heavy | **0.25–0.5 ha modular units**, not 40k ha ambition as step one |
| Costs can ramp until the model breaks | Public cost bands + **real quotes** + stop scenario on WHO |
| Opaque partnerships and private pain | **Public log**, registry, integrity rules |
| Community relationship is the durable asset | **Rumbacaca-first** intros; no cold extractive drop |
| “Make money in Moz as SA farmer” often fails | Pilot is **measured community model**, not another quiet commercial empire |
| Operator is not always in-country | **Don’t enroll households** into continuous support we can’t staff; remote HSE job is reality |
| Starlink / AI are tools | Never a cover story if farming fails again ([SYSTEM.md](SYSTEM.md)) |
