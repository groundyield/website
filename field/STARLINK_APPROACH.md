## 1. The short answer

**Do not** walk into a village school and say “we will give you Starlink” until hardware, licence, power, ownership, monthly fees, and a local education partner are real.

**Do** treat Starlink as a **connectivity pilot for schools + community hubs**, with GroundYield as the **on-the-ground implementer / documentation partner** — not as a Starlink reseller fantasy or a charity giveaway brand.

**Best model (what works elsewhere):**

```
Local education partner / ministry desk
        +
Starlink (connectivity product or program)
        +
Ground operator (you) who installs, trains, measures, publishes
```

Examples of the pattern (not GroundYield claims): school packages in Kenya (education org + national education centre + Starlink connectivity); rural school commitments discussed in other African markets. Those deals are **led by education institutions**, not by a one-day-old farm pilot alone.

## 3. Critical constraints (read before any meeting)

### Legal / regulatory

- Starlink has been **licensed / available in Mozambique** (public reporting since ~2022–23). **Confirm current availability** for the exact district on [starlink.com](https://www.starlink.com) map before promising service.
- **South Africa** is a different story (ongoing licence / ownership rules) — don’t plan SA school installs under MZ assumptions.
- Import, customs, and **who holds the subscription** (school vs NGO vs individual) matter for sustainability.

### Power

- Dish + router need **reliable power**. Rural schools often need solar + battery sized for night charge and cloudy days. Budget this as **harder than the dish**.

### Ownership after the pilot

Who pays month 13?

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| School / district pays | Local ownership | Often fails without budget line |
| Sponsor pays 12–24 months | Predictable pilot | Ends abruptly if sponsor leaves |
| Shared community hub (clinic/school) | More users per dish | Governance fights |

**Write this down before install.** Otherwise you create dependency and anger.

### GroundYield capacity (honest)

- Self-funded, **no legal entity yet** ([WHO.md](../WHO.md)) — enterprise partners may require an NGO/school co-signer.
- **Do not** scale promises beyond what one operator can install and support.
- Start: **1–3 school hubs**, not “every rural school in Inhambane.”

## 4. Best approach — sequence

### Phase 0 — Desk (now)

1. Confirm Starlink **map availability** for Vilanculos / target districts.  
2. Identify **1–2 rural schools** only after local intro (extension, district education, trusted contact) — not cold drop.  
3. Draft **one-page pilot concept** (below) — schools first, farm pilot secondary.  
4. Contact path:
   - Primary: [Starlink Business / Contact Sales](https://www.starlink.com/business) — education / community pilot  
   - Parallel: **district education / provincial education** (they open school doors)  
   - Optional: Mozambican education NGOs already doing digital literacy  

### Phase 1 — Local consent (on the ground, next 2 weeks)

5. Meet school leadership + community leaders **without promising Starlink**.  
6. Ask: power situation, existing connectivity, who would manage the kit, risks (theft, fees).  
7. Only if interested → “We will ask Starlink / partners; nothing is guaranteed.”  
8. Log in [CONTACT_LOG.md](CONTACT_LOG.md) + public one-liner in GROUND_TRIP if appropriate.

### Phase 2 — Formal ask to Starlink

9. Send short proposal (email below) with:
   - 1–3 candidate schools (names only with consent)  
   - Power plan sketch  
   - Metrics you will publish  
   - Ask: **hardware donation / education pricing / multi-site account** — pick one clear ask  
10. Copy a local education partner if you have one.

### Phase 3 — Only after written yes

11. Install, train 2 custodians, publish install log (cost, power, uptime, failures).  
12. Never announce “Starlink for all communities” on X until kit is on a roof.

## 5. What to ask Starlink for (pick one primary ask)

| Ask | When it fits |
|-----|----------------|
| **A. Education / community pricing** for 1–3 kits | Most realistic first step |
| **B. In-kind hardware for a documented pilot** (12 months service) | If they want a public African rural school case study |
| **C. Introduction to existing Africa education partners** | If they prefer not to deal with tiny pilots directly |
| **D. Technical guidance only** | Lowest friction; you fund kits yourself |

**Avoid:** “Sponsor our whole farm brand” · unlimited free dishes · political endorsement.

## 6. Metrics to offer (public)

If a pilot runs, publish monthly:

| Metric | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Uptime / outage days | Honesty |
| Power source & failures | Real rural constraint |
| Students/teachers with scheduled access hours | Not “connected” vanity |
| Incidents (theft, damage, abuse) | Integrity |
| Cost per month (who paid) | Replicability |

No fake student counts.
